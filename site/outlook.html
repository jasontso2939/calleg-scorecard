Bill Outlook

Estimate the chance a proposed bill is signed, from what is known when it is introduced. A statistical model trained on every legislator's bills from 2017-18 through 2025-26.

Read this as a rough guide. Trained on 2017-18 through 2023-24 and tested on the 2025-26 session it had not seen, the model ranked a signed bill above an unsigned one about of the time (AUC , where 0.5 is a coin flip). Estimates assume conditions like the 2025-26 session. It cannot see a bill's merits, the Governor's priorities, opposition, or deals made along the way.

What the model can and cannot see

It learns from five sessions (about 19,700 bills) and uses each author's record across them. It also uses committee rosters from the roll calls: whether the author sits on or chairs the committee. These added little accuracy beyond the author's party, record and the committee itself, so they move estimates only slightly. Sessions differ (2019-20 had far fewer bills signed because of the pandemic), so estimates are set to the level of the latest session. How members voted on the author's past bills is shown for context but left out of the estimate, because committee roll calls mostly record bills already headed for passage. Not yet included: bill text beyond the subject line, and support and opposition.
